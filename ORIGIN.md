## How civic issue-reporting became the seed

A ranking can be a lie. A landing page can be a costume. An issue report is harder to fake. Someone is standing somewhere, saying the pavement failed.

That pressure — **evidence in, map out, bilingual, no theatre** — is what the later studio kept:

1. **The city is a stream.** Not a deck. The bot taught ingestion before visualization. By 2026 the same instinct shows up as many pipes on one axis (the live-coding bible's first tactic: multi-source correlation).
2. **The channel matters.** LINE is not a “nice to have” in Thailand; it is where the report actually happens. The private `city-reporter-line-bot` is the ancestor of a studio that still ships bilingual (Thai welcome, English primary in these pages).
3. **Operations beat commentary.** A reporter that cannot route is a guestbook. Control towers, FloodDash, AirDash — they are what you build when you are tired of collecting issues into a void.
4. **Public method, private people.** Issue reports are PII-adjacent. The origin stayed private for the same reason `ikigai-dashboard` stayed private. The *method* did not have to stay private. Blueprints, rankings with formulas, and a flood gift-repo are how the seed went public without leaking the citizens who watered it.

The first *public* repository does not appear until **2026-02-09** (`slic-landing-page`). That is not a contradiction. SLIC is an argument about cities. The reporter is a practice inside one. The practice is older than the public argument. I will not fill the sixteen public-silent months with invented commits. I will only say: when the studio finally opened the door, it already knew how to listen.

## What a stranger should take from the origin

- Do not wait for a “smart city platform” RFP to start. A reporter bot is enough to begin.
- Do not publish the inbox. Publish the architecture.
- If you fork this studio, start from a real issue stream in a real geography — then put it on a map with sources you can name. That is the seed. The rest is grammar.
